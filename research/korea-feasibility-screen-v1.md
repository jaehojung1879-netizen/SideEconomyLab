# Korea Feasibility Screen v1 — Hard Gate

Date: 2026-10-03

## Decision

Before applying the 100-point opportunity score, screen the 30 normalized candidates against first-pilot constraints that can invalidate an idea regardless of theoretical market size.

This stage asks:

> Is this a sensible **first SideEconomy experiment** for a full-time employee, not “is this industry good in general?”

## Hard-gate dimensions

1. **First-pilot capital fit** — preferred KRW 3m–7m, normal ceiling about KRW 10m.
2. **Owner fit** — weekday interruption and emergency site work must be limited.
3. **Fixed-lease dependency** — standalone lease before demand validation is penalized heavily.
4. **Network dependency** — models requiring city-scale density are poor first pilots.
5. **Regulatory / operating complexity** — sanitation, safety, fraud, logistics and liability count as real costs.
6. **Reversibility** — movable/resaleable assets or asset-light tests are preferred.

### Examples supporting the gate

A Korean unmanned golf operator currently advertises a basic 15-pyeong setup at roughly KRW 30m before several site-specific costs. That is outside the current pilot capital rule.

By contrast, current Korean official-reseller listings for prosumer desktop 3D printers are roughly below KRW 1m to several million won, which leaves meaningful budget for materials and validation inside the pilot ceiling.

Seoul's official tourism site now lists dedicated unmanned luggage facilities in tourist districts, confirming real demand but also warning that a new locker operator faces visible incumbent supply.

## What this screen does NOT do

- no market-size ranking
- no 100-point score
- no revenue forecast
- no Seoul opportunity heatmap
- no pilot authorization

## Next research task

For the 12 KEEP candidates, collect C2 evidence:

- three Korean supplier/equipment quotes or public price points where applicable;
- real host economics / revenue-share examples where applicable;
- maintenance and service cadence;
- direct Korean competitors;
- licensing / tax / insurance flags;
- resale value or exit route;
- estimate of scheduled owner-hours and weekday interruption;
- a minimum viable paid field test.

Then apply the 100-point scoring framework to those 12 only.

The WATCH list can be promoted later if a specific evidence gap is cheaply resolved.
