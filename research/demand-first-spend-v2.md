## Findings
- DF-007: K-apt adds a concrete B2B spending source outside PPS. It exposes buyers, category, contract duration and sometimes explicit renewal. A narrow buyer-side or supplier-side renewal/calendar product is a testable hypothesis, not validated demand. Existing official search and Excel export are competitors, not assets we own. Research one nonregulated service, e.g. announcement services or nonregulated consumables; regulated inspection/security/disinfection is spending evidence only. No performance claim; expiration does not imply tender, churn or opportunity. Need 50 deduplicated detail records for one service, amendments/cancellation history and observation vintages, then holdout renewal timing vs a calendar baseline. Compare existing bid tools and free exports.
- DF-008: Two marketplace offers already cover reporting automation, data aggregation and scheduled execution at 30k–300k KRW asking prices. Both pages show zero reviews, so no demand or transaction-price conclusion. One seller offers weekday response and 3/14/30-day support, another advertises 30-day revisions and same-day/24h turnaround. Commodity custom work faces price competition and owner interruptions. No generic automation service build justified. Test a reusable single-industry product with strict input/output boundaries, scheduled support only and paid intent.
- DF-005: tenant-side renewal calendar is still exploratory; no new price evidence. DF-009: no matched resale transaction, no estimated margin. DF-002: no supplier net quote. Existing CV-022/CV-030 unchanged.

## Reverse economics
The new `pipeline/owner_capacity.py` computes necessary monthly integer billable units and maximum feasible units under explicitly specified cash costs, owner hours, and hypothetical capital recovery. It is independent of observed offers and never treats contract amounts as realizable revenue. All examples are hypothetical. Assumptions include uniform units, steady demand, fixed monthly hours and no income taxes or financing unless entered in costs. Initial build effort, acquisition, support, churn replacement and working-capital cost must be included in the user's inputs; no omitted costs may silently be treated as actual zeros. An owner-time limit or non-positive unit margin can disqualify a scenario even if demand is unknown. Passing a threshold proves only arithmetic consistency, not market fit.
- Three assumptions illustrate project-based vs reusable subscription vs low-margin brokerage. No forecast, score, new OC status or investment authorization.
- Real-world evidence remains zero. First paid test requires employer-policy and customer consent, separate approval.
